# Changelog

All notable changes to nas-backup-manager are documented in this file.

## [0.1.18]

### Fixed

- Each copy is written down as soon as it is in place, and a file of the destination identical to
  its original (size and time) is taken as its copy: a run stopped by a crash no longer makes `file
  (1)` duplicates nor stops updating a file. The folder is written to disk after each rename, so a
  copy lasts through a power cut.

### Changed

- What a run removes or replaces in the destination goes to the trash of its disk, a version already
  there kept beside it, numbered; while the trash is off, or on a network disk, it is removed as
  before.
- A scheduled or real-time run that would remove more than a quarter of the copies of a rule (at
  least 100) stops before removing any more and fails; a run started by hand removes them.
- A scheduled time missed while the NAS was off or asleep runs once at the start of the app or when
  the NAS wakes, with the notification "Backup eseguito in ritardo", instead of being skipped.
- The rule form says when source and destination are on the same disk.
- Real-time runs read the folders under a change through the index of the copies instead of the
  whole list.

### Security

- The socket of the app answers the launcher only (root, the owner of the folder of the sockets, or
  the app itself): another app of the NAS can no longer reach its API with headers it made up
  (`launcher_only.rs`).

## [0.1.16]

### Changed

- The Cloud section offers no new rule until a network disk is configured: the notice points to
  the disk manager, and a failed reading of the disks says so with a retry.
- The errors are short alerts with their sign, the options of the steps are switches, and the
  folders are named source and destination folder.
- The texts of the interface, in Italian and English, are impersonal and shorter: no ellipsis,
  colon or semicolon, no names of internal programs, commands or variables.
- The scrollbars come from the shared library, which shows only their thumb: no arrows and no
  track.

### Fixed

- The icon of the state of the last run (in progress, done, failed, interrupted, never) was
  missing in the build: PurgeCSS removed its classes, named at runtime, now kept.

### Removed

- An empty `CommonModal.vue`, the unused `lock.svg` icon, the unused text `columns.status` and
  comments that only named the markup below them.

## [0.1.13]

### Fixed

- On a NAS whose time zone is UTC the scheduled rules had no next run: systemd-analyze writes no
  `(in UTC)` line there, and its `Next elapse` is now read instead. The list showed "-" and the
  rules never started.
- 7-Zip gets the password of a new archive twice: the versions that ask to confirm it (p7zip
  16.02, as on Ubuntu 22.04) stopped with "password verification failed".

### Changed

- The trash of a disk (`.nas-trash`, nas-file-manager) is never backed up nor followed in real
  time.
- The files of `assets/`, with a hash of their content in the name, are served with
  `Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable`, the page with `no-cache`: the browser keeps
  the scripts and the styles between visits instead of asking for them again through the gateway
  and the launcher.
- The launcher bridge (under the adapter of `src/services/nasShell.ts`), the shared styles, the
  layout with its sidebar, the confirmation dialogs, the empty list, the power schedule ranges,
  the schedule texts and the build, package and release scripts come from the shared library
  `@valeriogc/nas-base-app`.

## [0.1.12]

### Added

- The list of the rules shows the kind of each schedule and its time ("Weekly · 03:00", the whole

- `GET api/volumes`: the names of the disks, from the registry of the launcher. The folders of a

## [0.1.11]

### Added

- The rules run for real. A scheduled rule runs at its time (`systemd-analyze calendar`, local time
  of the NAS; a missed time is skipped), a real-time one when its origin changes (`notify`,
  inotify), on the changed files only. Filters include or exclude files by extension, dates, size
  in MB or name; the rename adds its text before the name or before the extension; the archive is
  7z, AES-256, its names encrypted when asked, tested when asked, with the password on the input of
  7z. A mirror removes only the copies the rule made whose original is gone, never anything else; a
  copy never removes. Every copy is written aside and renamed into place, and checked byte by byte
  when asked.
- A rule has a name, can be turned on and off, edited and deleted; a real-time rule watches its
  origin and may write into it. The list shows the outcome of the last run (an icon, with date,
  time and reason on hover), the next run and the buttons to run or stop a rule now.
- Each run shows among the operations of the launcher, where it can be stopped; the application
  stays on while a scheduled or real-time rule is on (`run.background`), and asks for `7zip`.

### Changed

- The passwords of the rules never go back to the browser: a rule saved with an empty one keeps it.
- The rename preview puts the text after the name before the extension, as the rename does.
- Origin and destination cannot be one inside the other either way, and a mirror cannot take a
  whole disk as its destination.

- In the frame of the launcher the application keeps the rules of its old sandbox by itself
  (`src/services/frameGuard.ts`, called first in `main.ts`): no window of its own, no
  `alert`/`confirm`/`prompt` dialog, no navigation of the page that holds it. The launcher dropped
  the sandbox, which on its origin was no wall and made the browser warn: nothing else changes.
- The side menu lists Cloud last, after the scheduled and the real-time backups.

### Fixed

- The icons outside the buttons (the side menu, the closing crosses of the windows) follow the
  theme: they stayed black on the dark one.
